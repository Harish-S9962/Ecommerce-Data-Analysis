**E-Commerce Business Performance Report**

*Analysis of Orders, Revenue, Customers & Fulfillment (FY2024–2026)*

**1. Executive Summary**

The business processed 10,000 orders from 6,000 unique customers, generating ₹33.24 crore (₹332,439,944) in net revenue at an average order value of ₹33,244 and 30,164 units sold. Performance is broad-based across product categories, with no single category dominating — Fashion leads by a narrow margin. Growth is heavily concentrated in Tamil Nadu, which alone accounts for nearly half of all orders, pointing to a strong regional base rather than a nationally balanced footprint.

Regular customers drive the majority of volume and revenue, while a small VIP segment contributes a disproportionately high average order value. Fulfillment is solid, with over two-thirds of orders delivered successfully, though the combined Returned + Cancelled rate of ~14% merits attention. Revenue by year shows a slight downward trend from 2024 to 2025, with 2026 data reflecting a partial year.

**5. Customer Segment Analysis**

The customer base splits into Regular, Premium, and VIP tiers. Regular customers make up the bulk of both orders (64%) and revenue (64%), which is expected for a mass-market base. However, the real story is average spend per order:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Segment**        **Orders**       **Revenue**         **Avg. Order Value**
  ------------------ ---------------- ------------------- ----------------------
  Regular            6,423            ₹21,40,61,076       ₹33,327

  Premium            2,913            ₹9,67,55,223        ₹33,215

  VIP                664              ₹2,16,23,644        ₹32,566
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Notably, average order value is nearly flat across all three tiers (₹33,327 Regular, ₹33,215 Premium, ₹32,566 VIP) — the VIP label does not currently translate into a materially larger basket size. VIP customers also order far less frequently (only 664 orders, 6.6% of total). This suggests the segment is defined by something other than spend per transaction (e.g., lifetime value or manual assignment), and there is room to grow VIP order frequency through targeted engagement rather than relying on basket-size differences.

**6. Discounting Strategy**

Across all categories, discounts consume roughly 14% of gross category revenue on average. Fashion's above-average discount rate (14.5%) paired with its leading revenue suggests promotions are actively driving category leadership — a useful lever, but one to monitor for margin erosion. Electronics and Grocery, with the lowest discount ratios (~13.8%), are the most efficient categories from a promotional-spend perspective.

**12. Recommendations**

-   Reduce geographic concentration risk: Tamil Nadu drives ~48% of orders. Prioritize marketing and logistics investment in Maharashtra and Karnataka, the next-largest states, to build a more balanced revenue base.

-   Investigate the Returned + Cancelled rate (13.9% of orders, ₹4.48Cr revenue): break down by category and region to identify root causes such as sizing (Fashion), delivery delays, or product quality.

-   Re-evaluate Fashion's discount intensity: at 14.5% of revenue, it's the highest of any category — test whether a smaller discount sustains the same order volume to protect margin.

-   Grow VIP order frequency: VIP customers already spend competitively per order but transact far less often (664 orders vs. 6,423 for Regular). Loyalty or replenishment campaigns could increase frequency without discounting.

-   Fix the 'Unknown' city data gap: 40 orders lack a valid delivery city. Tightening address capture at checkout will improve the accuracy of future geographic analysis.

-   Investigate the 18-25 and 66+ demographic gaps: confirm whether the 66+ absence is a genuine customer gap or a data limitation, and consider targeted campaigns to lift 18-25 engagement, which currently lags other age bands by ~20-24% in orders.

-   Monitor the 2024→2025 flat trend closely as 2026 data completes: a second consecutive flat year would indicate a real plateau requiring a demand-generation response, not just a data artifact.

-   Explore basket-size growth over per-unit pricing: since revenue-per-unit is nearly uniform across categories, cross-sell and bundling are likely to move revenue more than price increases.

-   Close small data-capture gaps: 141 orders have an 'Unknown' gender and 40 have an 'Unknown' delivery city — tightening these fields at checkout/signup will sharpen future segmentation.
